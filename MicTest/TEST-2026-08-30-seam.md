## How the silent-room problem was solved

The previous report established that a Mac cannot dictate to itself: macOS voice processing
subtracts the machine's own speaker output from the input path, so `say` played aloud
produced `rms≈0.011` against a `0.010` floor and `partials=0`, at volume 69 and at 100. Its
conclusion — "needs a human at the mic, a second device, or a loopback audio driver" — missed
a fourth option.

The echo canceller only exists on the **speaker → microphone** path. Render the same speech to
a **file** and inject it *after* the tap, and the canceller is not in the circuit at all.

That is `MicTest/Sources/MicTest/SyntheticAudioSource.swift` (new, this session). It decodes an
audio file, converts it once to the real tap format, and feeds it in 1024-frame buffers on a
wall-clock schedule into `AppDelegate.processTap` — the single function every microphone buffer
already passes through.

```bash
say -v Kanya -o /tmp/thai.aiff -f MicTest/testdata/thai-continuous.txt   # ~134 s, no pauses
pkill -9 -f "MicTest.app/Contents/MacOS/MicTest"; : > /tmp/mictest_trace.txt
open -W -n -g --env MICTEST_AUTOSTART=1 --env MICTEST_AUTOSTART_HOLD=90 \
     --env MICTEST_AUDIO_FILE=/tmp/thai.aiff -a ~/Desktop/MicTest.app
grep -nE "rotation:|replay:|SYNTH:|FINAL:" /tmp/mictest_trace.txt
```

**The microphone deliberately stays open and its buffers are discarded in the tap closure.**
Skipping `installTap`/`e.start()` would have forked the start/stop state machine, and a
harness that runs a different lifecycle than the thing it tests is not a test. The cost is one
predicted branch on the audio thread; the benefit is that `frames`, `capturedTapFormat`, the
teardown path and the engine lifecycle are bit-for-bit the ones a real capture uses. Confirmed
in the trace: `frames=4355072` at 48 kHz = 90.7 s, exactly matching `SYNTH: stopped — fed 4253
buffers, 90.7 s of audio`. The microphone contributed nothing.

### The metric these tables use has since been fixed

Review finding 3 (below) is **now fixed in code**, after these four runs were recorded. Every
number in the tables above therefore predates the fix, and they are kept exactly as measured
rather than restated — a figure recorded under one definition should not be quietly reprinted
under another.

What changed. `beginFlushRotation` now latches the partial length into `Session.flushBaseline`
in the same critical section that sets `flushStartedAt` and records the replay window, and the
flushed-final line measures `+N` against that latched value instead of re-reading the live
`lastPartialLength` a beat later. Both trace lines now say `partial at flush` and quote the
same number by construction, and any movement between the flush and the final is reported
separately and with its sign:

```
rotation: flushing owner 1 at 20 s (endAudio); partial at flush len 172
rotation: flushed final after 137 ms, +4 chars beyond partial at flush
          (final 176 vs partial at flush 172); partial then moved +4 after the flush, to 176
```

That same seam printed `+0 (final 176 vs last partial 176)` under the old metric.

**Two consequences for the tables above, stated rather than assumed:**

- **Every `+N` is a lower bound.** The true recovery at each seam is `+N` plus however much
  the partial grew after that flush. That quantity *was* recorded — the flush line has always
  printed the flush-time baseline — so any seam whose raw trace survives can be corrected by
  arithmetic, without re-running anything. See "Run 4, corrected from its surviving trace"
  below. Runs 1–3 cannot be recovered: each run begins `: > /tmp/mictest_trace.txt`, so only
  the last run's trace still exists.
- **The two "contractions" are probably not contractions.** Run 2's `final 183 vs last partial
  184` and `final 186 vs last partial 189` report a last partial *longer than the final*,
  which is exactly what post-flush drift produces: the baseline climbed past the final after
  `endAudio()`. Under the corrected metric both may well be ordinary positive recoveries. The
  "5 extended, 2 contracted, 3 equal" count below should not be quoted until re-measured.

### Reading the table honestly

- **`+N > 0` at 3 of 6 seams is the result.** Those are characters the final carried that the
  last partial did not — precisely the text the seam was diagnosed as eating. This number had
  never been anything but zero before.
- **Flush latency 115–155 ms**, versus 33–34 ms in the silent-room runs. Not a regression: the
  earlier flushes had almost nothing to finalise. Still two orders of magnitude inside the
  plan's 2 s timeout.
- **`+0` is not one outcome, it is two, and the split matters.** Two of the five `+0` seams
  have a final SHORTER than the last partial (183 vs 184 and 186 vs 189, both in run 2): the
  recogniser revised *downward*. `+N` floors at 0, so the table prints `+0` and hides the
  direction. Counting properly, the ten seams are **5 extended, 2 contracted, 3 equal** — so
  the number of seams at which "there was nothing to recover" is **3 of 10, not 5 of 10**.
  *(Superseded by the section above: on the corrected baseline both "contractions" are most
  likely extensions, because a last partial longer than the final is the signature of the
  moving baseline rather than of a retraction. Re-measure before quoting this count.)* A contraction is not the fix
  failing: the retraction is reconciled by the injector, and run 2's `divergencesRepaired=27
  divergencesRefused=0` shows every one of those repairs landing.
- **The measurement is mostly but not fully reproducible.** Runs 1 and 3 — same audio, same
  50 s hold — agreed to the character at both seams. Run 2, at a 90 s hold, gave `+3` where
  they gave `+0` at the same 20 s seam on the same audio. So the pipeline is not chaotic, but
  a single seam is still not a measurement: something about the longer run moved it.
- **Run 1's `divergencesRefused=5` does not discount run 1's `+8`.** That refusal count is an
  artefact of focus drifting mid-run (see "Cautions" below), and it measures the *injector*.
  `+N` is computed where the flush's final meets the last partial — upstream of injection, and
  unaffected by which window the text eventually landed in. The largest recovery observed comes
  from the messier run, and that is fine.

## What this does not prove

Stated plainly, because the previous report's central lesson was that a green number is not a
proof:

1. **The voice is synthetic.** `say -v Kanya` is text-to-speech. It has no disfluencies, no
   breath, no variable rate, and unnaturally clean phoneme boundaries. Apple's recogniser may
   well segment it differently than it segments a person. **A `+N > 0` here proves the seam
   recovers audio that crosses it; it does not prove that a sentence a human says survives.**
   Plan verification item 6 still needs a human.
2. **The microphone and `AVAudioEngine`'s input node are bypassed** by construction. Nothing
   here re-tests them; the previous report's runs did.
3. **Thai accuracy is measured against a synthetic reading of a text this session wrote**, not
   against natural speech. It came out very close (see below), which says the pipeline is
   sound, not that field accuracy is known.

## Code review of the uncommitted work

A review pass over the whole uncommitted body (~3,700 insertions, never reviewed as a whole)
produced four findings. Two were in code this session added and are **fixed and verified**;
two are in inherited code and are left for a decision.

**Fixed**

1. **`SyntheticAudioSource` delivered one buffer after `stop()`.** The pacing loop checked its
   stop flag before sleeping but not after, so a `stop()` landing mid-sleep still produced one
   more `processTap` call into a torn-down capture. Not theoretical — it was visible in every
   run as a 1,024-frame gap between `capture stopped … frames=2433024` and `AUTOSTART SUMMARY
   … frames=2434048`. Fixed by re-reading the flag after the sleep; run 4 reports
   **delta 0** and one fewer buffer fed. It never corrupted a measurement (`append` tolerates
   a retired session) but it made the harness's frame count differ from the microphone path it
   claims to be indistinguishable from, which is the one property the harness sells.
2. **`MICTEST_AUTOSTART_HOLD=nan` defeated the clamp.** `TimeInterval("nan")` parses
   *successfully* to NaN, and NaN passes through `min(max(x, 1.0), 600.0)` untouched because
   every comparison against NaN is false. The result was NaN timer intervals for all three
   scheduled selectors, with neither the "is not a number" warning (`TimeInterval("nan") ==
   nil` is false) nor the sub-20 s warning (`nan < 20.0` is false) firing — a run that
   schedules nothing and says nothing. Verified against the real expression before fixing.
   Now gated on `.isFinite`. (`inf` was already handled by the ceiling.)

**Fixed in a later pass — inherited code, both now closed**

Findings 3 and 4 were left open by the review and have since been fixed. Both are
**verified by a clean `-swift-version 6 -O -wmo` build and by reading, not by a measured run**
— see the caveat under finding 3.

3. **`LiveRecognizer.swift:940` — `+N` is measured against a baseline that moves, and two
   trace lines use the same label for different values.** `beginFlushRotation` captures
   `lastPartialLength` and prints `last partial len 172`; the final path re-reads the *same
   property* at line 940 and prints `vs last partial 176`, because partials keep arriving in
   the documented append-after-`endAudio()` window. So `+0 (final 176 vs last partial 176)`
   was reported for a seam whose flush-time baseline was 172 — four recovered characters made
   invisible. **This is very likely the explanation for the run-to-run variation recorded
   above**, and it means every `+N` in this report is a lower bound. Worth fixing before the
   number is quoted again; it changes what the metric means, so it wants its own run.

   **FIXED.** `Session.flushBaseline` now latches the partial length in the same critical
   section that sets `flushStartedAt` and records the replay window, and the final measures
   against that instead of re-reading the live property. Both lines print `partial at flush`
   and quote the same number by construction; movement between flush and final is reported
   separately, with its sign.

   **Partly verified without a re-run.** The flush line has always printed the flush-time
   baseline, so a surviving trace carries both numbers and the correction is arithmetic. Run
   4's trace survived: its seams correct from `+0` and `+5` to **`+4` and `+8`**, confirming
   the finding against the primary source. The other eight seams' traces are gone (each run
   truncates the trace file), so those figures stay as measured and stay lower bounds; the
   harness re-run that would refresh them is still outstanding, for the reasons under
   "Runs 1–3 not re-measured, deliberately".
4. **`TextInjector.swift:558` — a 40 ms `usleep` on the main actor.** `inject` is
   `@MainActor` and `main.swift:4162` enables the standing-selection path for every partial
   after the first, so when a selection is present and the field lags, dictation stalls the
   actor that drives typing for longer than one 30 Hz tick. Same pattern at lines 657 and
   1370.

   **FIXED, at all three sites.** Each was an unconditional 40 ms block followed by exactly
   one re-read; each is now the file's own early-exit poll (`selection(of:becomes:within:)` at
   558 and 1370, `selection(of:movesOff:within:)` at 657) against a new
   `axAsyncApplySettleTimeout = 0.04`. Stated exactly, because "the same 40 ms ceiling" would
   be wrong: `settledSelection` checks its deadline *before* sleeping, so against a 25 ms poll
   interval this budget reads at t=0, 25 and 50 ms. A healthy field costs **1 round trip and
   0 ms** (was 2 and 40 ms); a field that never settles costs **3 round trips and ~50 ms** (was
   2 and 40 ms). The common case — every partial that finds a standing selection — gets the
   full 40 ms back, and the wedged case pays 10 ms and one round trip more. Not lowered to
   0.025 (which would give a 25 ms / 2 round-trip worst case) because that moves the last read
   to t=25 and would newly fail a field applying between 25 and 40 ms — at the `movesOff` site
   that means pasting the user's text twice. Latency was the thing to trade; detection was not.

   **The blocking itself was kept deliberately.** Making these paths `async` to free the main
   actor would allow other main-actor work to interleave mid read-modify-write against the
   focused element, and the side effects `settledSelection` documents ("a pending clipboard
   restore from a PREVIOUS injection cannot run while we are here") depend on precisely that
   not happening. Shortening the stall is the fix; yielding the actor would have been a
   correctness regression wearing a latency win. 657 also keeps its explicit nil branch: a
   failed read must still trust the `.success`, because treating it as a no-op would route a
   landed AX insertion to the clipboard fallback and paste the user's text twice.
